# Prompt 02 — Premium Group Travel Landing Page

Design and code the landing page for **Wildpack**, an invented travel brand that runs small-group trips (8 to 14 people) where strangers set off together and come back as friends. Think sunrise hikes in the Dolomites, sailing week in the Greek islands, a food crawl through Tokyo, a desert camp in Morocco.

Who it's for: people in their late 20s to early 40s who have the budget for something special but don't want to plan it alone, and who care more about the shared stories than about ticking off sights. The page has one job: make them want to pick a trip and reserve a spot.

Tone and look: premium but playful. It should feel like the invitation to the best trip of the year, with energy, warmth and a bit of humor, never like a corporate travel agency. Come up with a strong, recognizable brand personality (color palette, type pairing, graphic details) and write short, lively copy that sounds like real people.

Sections to include: an opening that sells the feeling in one glance, a browsable set of upcoming trips (destination, dates, group size, seats left, price), a "how it works" explanation of the group format, a glimpse of the group vibe and past travelers, a few answers to common hesitations (traveling solo, age range, what's included), and a final push to book. The page must look great from phone to large desktop, with purposeful animations and interactive states.

Functionality: the menu links must scroll to their sections, the trip list must be filterable or sortable in some real way, and choosing "Reserve a spot" on a trip should open a mock reservation flow (trip already selected, number of travelers, contact details, confirmation screen). No real payments.

All visuals (hero, each destination, group moments, anything else the page needs) must be created with OpenAI's GPT Image 2.5 model, run through the Codex CLI using my existing Codex subscription (no separate API key, no other image generator). Don't use stock images, placeholder services or empty boxes.

When you're done, launch a local preview so I can see the page running. Layout, stack and architecture are up to you.
